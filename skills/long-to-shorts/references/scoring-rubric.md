# Scoring rubric for short clips

Score each candidate on five parts, 0 to 20 each, total 100. Be strict. Most of a long video should score below 60, and that is fine.

## 1. Hook strength (0 to 20)

How strongly the first 3 seconds make a stranger stay.

- 17 to 20: a specific claim, a surprise, a question the viewer wants answered, a mistake, a number or a clear contrast. Example: "I lost forty thousand dollars before I learned this."
- 11 to 16: interesting but needs a moment to land.
- 5 to 10: warm-up, greeting, context first.
- 0 to 4: "so um, yeah, today we are going to..."

You can fix a weak start by moving the start forward to the strongest sentence. You cannot invent a hook that is not in the words.

## 2. Self-contained story (0 to 20)

Can someone who has seen nothing else follow and enjoy it?

- Has a setup, a turn and a landing, even if each is one sentence.
- Does not rely on "as I said before" or on a slide or product the viewer cannot see.
- Names people and things clearly enough. If a key name is only said earlier, the clip needs the earlier line or loses points.

## 3. Emotion (0 to 20)

Does it make people feel something? Laughter, surprise, curiosity, anger, awe, a useful "oh, I get it". Flat information scores low unless the insight is sharp. Real energy in the voice matters; read the delivery in the transcript (short bursts, laughter, interruptions) and in the picture.

## 4. Payoff (0 to 20)

Does it end on something? A punchline, an answer, a clear takeaway, a strong final line, a cliffhanger that is honest about being one. A clip that trails off, or ends mid-argument, loses most of this score.

## 5. Length fit (0 to 20)

- 30 to 60 seconds: full marks for most platforms.
- 20 to 30: full marks only if the clip is unusually tight.
- 60 to 90: full marks only when the story earns every second; otherwise 10 to 14.
- Over 90: cap. Do not exceed unless the creator asks.
- Under 15: usually too thin.

Platform hints: TikTok and Reels favor 20 to 60 seconds. YouTube Shorts allows up to 3 minutes but retention drops fast, so keep to 60. If the creator names a length, that wins.

## Calibration

- 90 to 100: share-worthy. Rare. Maybe one per hour of video.
- 70 to 89: strong and complete. Ship it.
- 50 to 69: usable, one clear weakness. Include only to reach a requested count, and say what the weakness is.
- Below 50: omit.

## Picking edges

- Start on the first word of the strongest sentence, not on a filler ("so", "um", "right"). Cut a leading "and" or "but" only if it does not change meaning.
- End on the last word of the payoff, plus about 0.15 s of air. If the speaker repeats the point after, cut before the repeat.
- Never cut mid-word or mid-clause. Use the word timings, not guesses.
- If the best part needs one sentence of setup from earlier, either include it when the clip stays short enough, or rewrite nothing and drop the clip.
- Two nearby segments may join in one clip only when they are one continuous thought. Do not stitch unrelated quotes together.

## Bad candidates to avoid

- Housekeeping: intros, sponsor reads, "like and subscribe", scheduling talk.
- Long lists with no turn.
- Answers to a question that was never heard.
- Heavy jargon with no explanation.
- Two people talking over each other.
- A quote that changes meaning once cut. If in doubt, drop it.
- Private or sensitive information about people that the speaker would not want clipped. Ask the creator.

## What to record per candidate

`number`, `title`, `sourceStart`, `sourceEnd`, `duration`, the five part scores, `total`, `hook` (speaker's words), `reason` (one concrete sentence tied to the actual content), `risk` (anything to double check). Save this in the director brief.

## Worked example

A 50 minute interview. Candidate at 18:42 to 19:31 (49 s): the guest says a number everyone gets wrong, explains why in three sentences, and ends with a joke.

Hook 18, story 17, emotion 16, payoff 18, length 20 = 89. Reason: opens with a specific wrong-number claim, explains it in plain words, ends on a laugh.

Another candidate at 7:10 to 8:20 (70 s): a story with a good ending but a slow start. Hook 8, story 15, emotion 14, payoff 17, length 12 = 66. Move the start to the sentence where it gets interesting (7:38). It becomes 42 s and the hook score rises to 14, length to 20, total 80.
