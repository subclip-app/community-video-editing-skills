# The six-block shot prompt

Keep blocks 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 identical for every shot in a set. Only block 2 changes.

1. **Anchor.** Say what each reference is: image 1 is the scene (one sentence: who, where, what they hold, what is behind them, what time), image 2 is the face, and face and hair must match it exactly. Say it is one continuous shot of this same moment in this same place.
2. **Camera.** Where the camera is (height, distance, direction, any object it is inside or behind), what it sees in the foreground and background, and where the person sits in the frame. This is the only part that creates the angle. Write it as a position, not a style word.
3. **Subject action.** Small living details only: a slow breath, a blink, a thumb moving on an object. No big actions, so the angle stays the star and the face stays stable. Where an angle needs it (profile, close-up), one small deliberate action such as a sip or one slow blink.
4. **Light and grade.** Name the real light sources in the base frame (ceiling panels, window glow, a screen) and the colour grade. Add: photorealistic, a real cinema camera, natural film grain. Repeating this block keeps the grade the same across the set.
5. **Sound.** Describe the room tone in a few words, or write that there is no music. Leave dialogue out.
6. **Camera behaviour.** State that the camera itself makes no sound and, for a locked shot, does not move. Add a slow drift only if you want one.

## Example skeleton (fill the brackets)

Image 1 is the scene: [person] sits on [seat] in [place] at [time], holding [object], [background]. Image 2 is their face: face and hair match it exactly. One continuous shot of this same moment in this same place.

[CAMERA BLOCK FOR THIS SHOT]

They stay [pose], relaxed, with small living details only: a slow breath, a blink, [one tiny hand detail].

The light of image 1: [sources], [grade]. Photorealistic, real cinema camera, natural film grain.

The natural sound of the room: [tone]. The camera itself is silent.

## Adapting to your scene

- Swap every object for ones that really are in your base frame. If the shot needs an object you do not have (a mirror, a cart, blinds), generate a new base frame that includes it, or choose a different angle.
- Close-ups and macro need a small, readable object in the base frame.
- Keep one person only. Add "no other people" if extras appear.
- If the face drifts, move the face photo to image 1 priority in the wording and shorten block 3.
