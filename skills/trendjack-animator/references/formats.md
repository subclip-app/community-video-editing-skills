# Formats

Pick the format by product. Emotional everyday products: drama song or narrated story. A visible mechanism: 3D explainer. Body comfort or posture: skeleton. Impulse or fun: product song. A real creator or founder video to copy: photoreal clone.

| Format | Audio | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drama song ad: sung mini-story, sting, close person brings it, the win | song | 15 to 20 s |
| Product song or music video: catchy chorus, fast cuts on the beat | song | 15 to 45 s |
| Narrated cartoon story: problem, trusted person, how it works, payoff | voice and music | 20 to 45 s |
| 3D how-it-works explainer on a studio stage | voice and music | 15 to 30 s |
| Skeleton or X-ray demo | voice or song | 15 to 30 s |
| Photoreal phone-footage clone of a real creator or founder video | voice | 15 to 40 s |

Sung or spoken is decided from the format and, when copying a reference, from the reference's audio, never from a brief that says "VO". Rhyming lines with stretched words are sung. If unsure, ask the creator.

## 1. Drama song ad (sung, 15 to 20 s)

A tiny sales story, sung in the first person. Someone close says something that stings, the narrator says why it happens, a trusted person brings the product, and the same people at the same place see the change.

| # | Beat | What the line does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The sting | Someone close says something that stings, as a quote | "Grandpa skipped a page!" she laughed. |
| 2 | The cause | Why it happens, plain and specific | The words were just a blur. |
| 3 | The mentor | A close, trusted person brings the product | My son came with a lamp. |
| 4 | The truth | One line about how it works, with a key word | "It's bright. That's the point." |
| 5 | The rise | The change, said simply and honestly | Now I read every word. |
| 6 | The win | Same place, same witness, the opposite of line 1 | "Read it again!" she begs. |
| 7 | The offer | Product name and offer | Try Acme for thirty nights. |

- The win only lands if line 6 happens where line 1 did, in front of the same person.
- The key word ("bright") carries how the product works, ideally with a second meaning.
- The hero looks like the buyer (age, gender, world). The witness is someone the buyer loves: grandchildren, a partner, a friend, a colleague.
- Pitch three arcs on one card with their lines; recommend the one whose hero is most like the buyer.
- Lyrics: 7 lines, about 30 words for 15 s, 3 to 7 words per line, one idea per line, no rhymes, no metaphors, everyday speech. Other people's quotes carry the sting and the win; the narrator reports.
- Lip-sync: the hero may sing 1 or 2 lines on camera (best: line 1 reacting to the sting, or line 5 the rise). The quoted lines of other people are acted, not lip-synced.
- Write the brand name as it should be sung, and fix the spelling in the captions afterwards.
- Song: warm uplifting pop, acoustic pop or country-pop at 105 to 120 bpm, the hero's vocal gender, sung in the target language. Never slow or sad: a ballad kills a short ad.
- Shots: one per phrase; split a long first line into the quote and "she laughed". The product first appears in the mentor shot. The last shot ends on the product.

## 2. Product song or music video (sung, 15 to 45 s)

A short catchy original song about the moment the product fixes, cut like a music video: one quick shot per phrase, on the beat. Less story, more rhythm and repetition.

| Part | Length | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook line | first 2 s | The problem or a vivid picture, sung from the first word: Cold feet at midnight, again |
| Verse | 2 to 4 lines | The everyday moment, specific and visual |
| Chorus | 2 lines, sung twice | The promise or reframe, simple to remember: It was never you / it was your old pillow |
| Feature line | 1 line | The one thing that makes it work, said plainly |
| Offer | 1 line | Product name and offer |

- 3 to 7 words per line. Repetition is the point: the chorus comes back word for word. A natural rhyme is fine; never force one. One feature line at most.
- Genre by buyer: broad 25 to 45, upbeat pop 110 to 120 bpm. 45 and up, home and family: warm country-pop or acoustic pop 100 to 115. Calm categories (sleep, wellbeing): warm acoustic pop 100 to 110 with a steady beat. Young and playful: bright pop with handclaps 115 to 125. Avoid genres that bury words (dance, house, EDM, K-pop). Never slow or sad.
- Shots: median 1.5 to 2.5 s, a new picture on every phrase, cuts on the first word of a phrase. Each chorus pass gets different shots (never reuse a shot). Characters act the words. The hero gets 1 or 2 lip-sync shots on the chorus.

## 3. Narrated cartoon story (spoken, 20 to 45 s)

An animated short with a warm narrator: a relatable problem, a person the hero trusts, a short look at how the product works, the payoff.

| Beat | Share | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | first 1 to 3 s | Tension or curiosity in the first sentence, on a close shot. Never a calm establishing shot |
| Problem | about 15 percent | Specific and sensory (3 a.m., the cold side of the bed, the stiff back at the desk) |
| Self-blame | 1 line | "I thought it was just me." The emotional hinge |
| Failed fixes | about 10 percent | A fast montage of 1 to 1.5 s inserts of what did not work |
| The person | about 10 percent | Someone the hero trusts shows the answer |
| How it works | 1 or 2 lines | One clear mechanism, shown while it is said |
| Payoff | about 15 percent | The first good result, ideally a callback to the hook |
| Twist | 1 line | Sweet or funny, never a reveal of wrongdoing or loss |
| Offer | last 2 or 3 lines | Product name, the offer, the next step |

- One narrator, first person or warm third person. Short sentences, one per line. ONE continuous voiceover of all lines together.
- If the narrator is on screen, their talking shots are lip-sync shots.
- If the voice service fails, wait and retry rather than switching to a sung voice.
- Music bed: instrumental, steady and light, low under the voice. Founder and talking ads usually work better with no bed at all; ask.
- One shot per line (split long lines). Show the mechanism literally while it is said.

## 4. 3D how-it-works explainer (spoken, 15 to 30 s)

A curious question, then one smooth camera journey that shows how the product works from the inside, on a clean studio stage, told by a calm, matter-of-fact narrator. Best for products with a visible mechanism (a filter, a pump, layered fabric, a hidden compartment).

Beats (one shot each, 4 to 8 shots):

1. The question (hook): "Why do houseplants die while you're away?" The everyday object on the stage, close.
2. The surprise: the real reason, in one plain sentence.
3. Into the object: the camera pushes into a cutaway or cross-section.
4. The mechanism: the part that does the work, animated simply (water rising, layers compressing, air flowing).
5. Back out: the result in the real world.
6. The product: name and offer, product as the hero on the stage.

- One seamless studio stage for the whole ad: a curved solid backdrop in one colour, soft studio light, everything on the stage floor. Describe it the same way in every still, or make one empty stage image and attach it as a place plate.
- Look: 3D animated film or glossy 3D product render. Cutaways are clean and simple, no labels or text in the image.
- Voice: calm, dry, short sentences, no hype words. Music: playful light instrumental (plucked bass or pizzicato, soft ticking pulse, about 95 bpm, curious, no vocals), low under the voice. Do not imitate a specific creator's voice, music or branding.
- Camera: every shot moves and the moves connect (push in, push through the surface, glide along the mechanism, pull back). Keep the screen direction the same so it feels like one journey.

## 5. Skeleton or X-ray demo (spoken or sung, 15 to 30 s)

A friendly cartoon skeleton (or an X-ray view of a character) shows what is going on inside: the slumped spine at the desk, the knees on hard floors, the neck on the wrong pillow. Then the product fixes the picture. Good for posture, footwear, pillows, chairs, bags.

1. Hook: the skeleton in an everyday pose that is obviously wrong, close, with a comic line ("This is your spine on a 9-hour workday.").
2. Exaggerate: two or three quick shots of it getting worse (comic, never gory).
3. The swap: the product arrives (slides in, is handed over, drops into frame).
4. The fix: same pose, now aligned or relaxed, with a glowing highlight where it changed.
5. Offer: product hero shot, name and offer.

Rules: a cute rounded cartoon skeleton with big eyes, no realistic bones, blood or pain faces. No medical claims: say only what the product page supports. Same skeleton in every shot, with its own character plate and an identifying prop (a striped scarf). X-ray variant: a normal character with a glowing see-through overlay on the body part that matters.

## 6. Photoreal phone-footage clone (spoken, 15 to 40 s)

An AI version of a real creator or founder video. Take the reference footage's shot list (its lines and cuts). Make photoreal stills with the real product photo as reference and the same hand, skin and setting words in every prompt. Keep the top 18 percent and bottom 25 percent of each still free for the interface and captions. Then animate each still with a start-frame video model: short shots, handheld camera moves. Do not copy a real person's face or voice: use a new person and your own words. Use the `phone-footage` look.
