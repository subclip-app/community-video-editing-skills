# Looks

Put the chosen look line in the project brief. It is added to every plate, still and clip prompt, so describe it once, in words, and never name a studio, film or franchise. Show these options on a card before writing prompts; the default is the 3D animated film.

| Look | Look line | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3D animated film (default) | 3D animated feature film, stylized CGI in the look of a modern big-studio animated movie: soft subsurface skin, big expressive eyes, appealing rounded shapes, cinematic lighting, highly polished render. | Stories with people, family, emotion | Faces drifting: always attach the plates |
| Glossy 3D product render | Clean glossy 3D product visualization, studio lighting with soft reflections, seamless curved backdrop, crisp materials, smooth simple shapes, premium commercial render. | Explainers, cutaways, mechanisms | Too sterile for stories; add a character only if needed |
| Claymation | Handmade stop-motion claymation: plasticine characters with visible fingerprints and tool marks, miniature handmade set with felt and wood props, soft practical lighting, shallow depth of field. | Warm, funny, nostalgic | Smooth motion breaks the illusion: ask for "slightly choppy stop-motion movement" |
| Watercolor | Hand-painted watercolor illustration on textured cold-press paper: translucent washes, soft pigment blooms, visible paper grain, loose ink linework, warm limited palette. | Gentle, emotional, wellbeing, gifts | Fast action smears; keep moves slow and simple |
| Paper cut-out | Layered paper-craft diorama: cut cardstock shapes with visible paper fibres, soft drop shadows between layers, handmade miniature world, warm light. | Storybook, kids and family, simple steps | Keep it to 2 to 4 depth layers; busy scenes turn to clutter |
| Wool or felt | Cosy handmade knitted and crocheted world: felted wool characters, visible stitches and yarn fibres, soft toy-like set, warm light. | Cosy, home, winter, kids | Small details get lost; frame closer |
| Anime or comic | Hand-drawn anime-style cel-shaded illustration: clean confident linework, flat colours with soft gradients, expressive faces, speed lines for action, original characters. | Young audiences, energetic hooks | Never copy known characters; keep line weight consistent |
| Editorial collage | Editorial paper collage: cut-out photographs and textured paper shapes, halftone dots, torn edges, bold limited palette, layered magazine style. | Explainers, news-style hooks, myths vs facts | No invented numbers or charts; keep text out of the images |
| Motion graphics | Clean flat vector motion graphics: simple geometric shapes, bold limited palette, smooth easing, minimal icons, lots of negative space. | Step-by-step, comparisons, how-to | Only real numbers; captions carry the words, not the images. A fully geometric look can be built as a HyperFrames layer instead of generated clips |
| Photoreal phone footage | Realistic handheld smartphone video: natural daylight, slight grain, true-to-life skin and materials, shallow depth of field, no studio polish. | Clones of real creator or founder footage, product-in-hand shots | Faces and hands drift most; attach the real product photo and repeat the hand and setting words in every prompt |

For more named art styles, the community skill `ai-style-atlas` has 86 looks with recipes; use its style card as the look line.

## Look notes

- Knitted and felt looks default to a white studio background: say the room fills the frame edge to edge ("walls fill the whole background, no white background"), and for a night scene that it is almost completely dark.
- A look that animates badly (parchment, paper): two stills (before and mid-action, the second made with the first as reference) cut together look right and cost less than a clip.
- Same scene in many styles (a style showcase reel): every prompt carries the same person, room and action words; only the style block changes. The start frame is the moment just before the action, the clip is the action.

## Motion lines

Every clip prompt opens with the camera, then one action: `Camera: slow push-in from a medium shot to a close-up of her face. She looks up from the book and smiles.` Useful moves: push-in, pull-back, orbit 30 to 90 degrees, crane down, top-down tracking, dolly through a doorway, rack focus from hands to face, low dolly toward the product. Never "static camera".
