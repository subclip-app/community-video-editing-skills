# Phase 1: Angles

An ad is three levers, in this order of impact: the angle (who you talk to and which pain or desire you enter through), the offer, then the creative. A strong angle carries a plain edit. A weak angle is not saved by a pretty one. So the angle is the one thing the creator decides.

Deliver the four blocks below in one message, then stop at the gate.

## 1. Voice of customer

List what the buyer would say, in their own words, not marketing words.

- Pains: at least 5. "I have five tabs open and still posted nothing" beats "workflow fragmentation".
- Desires: at least 5.
- Objections: at least 3 ("it is too small", "I already tried one").

If the creator gave reviews, comments or links, copy short literal phrases and mark them `verbatim`. Otherwise this is your hypothesis: say so. Good ads are transcribed from real buyers, not invented. Where to look when real text exists: three-star reviews (honest about good and bad), five-star reviews (the language of a met desire), comments under ads in the niche, forum threads, competitors' reviews.

## 2. The angle table

Give 8 to 12 numbered angles so the creator can answer "3". Each row: number, type, a one-line hook the avatar can say aloud in under 4 seconds, and one line on what makes it land.

Types to cover (pick the ones that fit, not all):

| Type | Enters through |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | the thing that annoys them now |
| Identity | who they are or want to be |
| Mechanism | why the problem happens and why this fixes it |
| Benefit | the after picture |
| Objection-breaker | the doubt that stalls the purchase |
| Emotional | a feeling, not a fact |
| Status | what using it says about them |
| Fear or loss | what they lose by waiting |
| Convenience | how easy or fast it is |
| Price or value | what it saves or how far it goes |
| Comparison | the alternative they use today |
| Myth-buster | a belief you take apart |

Two angles that only differ by wording are one angle. If you cannot say in one sentence why two rows land differently on the same person, cut one. A hook that only works as written copy will not survive spoken delivery: cut it.

## 3. The proposed mechanism

Name the culprit behind the problem and why the product breaks it, in words a child would follow. A promise without a reason is a hope; a promise with a reason reads as a conclusion. Give the problem a simple villain (a habit, a design flaw, a myth) and a picture the avatar can show with her hands. If the mechanism is good, make sure it is also a row in the table.

## 4. Awareness level

Pick one and say why in a line.

| Level | The viewer | The ad does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | does not know they have the problem | opens their eyes with a story |
| Problem-aware | feels it, sees no fix | agitates, then shows a way out |
| Solution-aware | knows fixes exist, not yours | explains the mechanism |
| Product-aware | knows you, has doubts | proof, comparison, objections |
| Most aware | wants it | offer, deadline, guarantee, straight to the call to action |

Cold traffic enters through problem or mechanism, never "buy now". Offer and urgency belong to warm and retargeting audiences. Most markets are crowded, so mechanism and identity usually beat a louder promise.

Flag the market, the language and the offer as `[assumption]` if you guessed them.

## End with exactly this line, then stop

> Which angle should I develop? (tell me the number, or say pick the best one)

If there is no avatar image yet, ask for it in the same message: send one, or say you should make a fictional one.

When the creator says "do it all in one go", still write the table, name the angle you picked and why, then continue.
