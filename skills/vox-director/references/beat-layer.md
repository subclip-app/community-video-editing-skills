# Beat and shot design

A beat delivers one thought. A shot shows one view of it. For a 60-second piece try six narration beats and about twelve shots. For 30 seconds, try six to eight visual changes across three or four longer narration beats. Start near 130 to 150 spoken words per minute, then time the actual delivery. A continuous take can run across multiple shots.

| Arc | Shot planning order |
| --- | --- |
| Hook and payoff | Hook, context, build, payoff, button |
| How it works | Hook, name the mechanism, show two or three steps, show the benefit |
| Timeline | Start, events, turning point, present, takeaway |
| Myth buster | Lead with the fact, name the myth, show the error, restate the fact |
| List | Promise, items, strongest item, recap |
| Problem, agitate, solve | Show pain, raise stakes, show the fix and proof, call to action |
| Before, after, bridge | Show contrast, demonstrate what changes, call to action |
| Attention, interest, desire, action | Interrupt, explain, show benefit, offer the next step |
| StoryBrand | Customer wants something, faces a problem, finds a guide and plan, acts |
| Fall and recovery | Stable world, setback, deeper trouble, climb out, improved state |
| Story spine | Once, everyday life, one change, consequences, resolution, new normal |
| Origin | Old world, spark, leap, struggle, breakthrough, today |
| Three act | Setup, rising confrontation, resolution |
| Story circle | Comfort, need, departure, search, discovery, cost, return, change |

## Coverage

- Hook quickly with a verified surprise, direct question, demonstrable mistake, experiment or outcome tease. Show the hook before supplying context.
- Try a three-to-six-second shot. On a longer beat, establish the scene with a wide, then cut to the detail on the revealing word or action. Put the short headline on the wide once. A short or emotional beat can keep one shot.
- Use an establishing wide for geography, medium for process, close for emotion, and extreme detail for a texture, mark or number. Move progressively closer to build pressure. Pull out to reveal context.
- Assign one camera move per shot, with separate directions for the visible pieces. Alternate scale, translation and stillness. A timeline can pan consistently in one direction, push in on its turning point and pull out on its takeaway.
- Preserve flat paper with uniform scale, lateral or vertical translation, or shallow parallax. Layer several rigid motions on a wide, then isolate one action in the close. Save a traveling bird, coin or arrow for a beat that earns the accent. Keep the payoff still long enough to read.
- An orbit, roll or simulated perspective move can distort flat art. Use an approved experimental shot with no critical lettering and compare a flat version. For a whip, make the change at the cut and verify the extra overlap against the narration.
- End on the payoff, give one short action if the format requires it, or mirror the opening for a loop.

## Example beat map

**Beat 2, 05.0 to 13.5.** “The crane lifts the whole box. At the dock, nobody has to unload it one sack at a time.”

| Time | Size | Headline | Camera | Pieces |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05.0 to 09.1 | Wide | ONE LIFT | Small lateral pan | Crane hook rises, foreground rail drifts, paper waves settle |
| 09.1 to 13.5 | Detail | | Locked | Container latch pivots on “At the dock” and settles, sacks remain still |

Cut on the spoken reveal. Keep both shots in the same newsprint theme and use the accent to lead the eye from crane to latch.
