# Themes and prompts

Choose a theme for the subject's place, period and tone. A film about Chinese woodblock printing can keep its ink and vermilion in any language. Compare three or four themes on the same composition. Keep the winner's paper edges, grain, type and shadow treatment consistent. A color progression can signal time or emotion without changing the print treatment.

| Theme | Palette and type | Finish and motion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| American retro | Pulp primaries and chunky slab | Aged halftone, punchy |
| Swiss modern | Two colors with red accent, bold grotesk | Modular grid and fine grain, calm |
| Punk zine | Black and white plus one spot color, cut-out letters | Photocopy and misregistration, punchy |
| Constructivist | Red, black and cream, diagonally set condensed type | Newsprint and letterpress, punchy |
| WPA poster | Three muted inks and stencilled type | Screenprint grain, calm |

| Chinese ink | Ink and vermilion, brush lettering and seal | Woodblock on rice paper, calm |
| Atomic age | Teal, orange and cream, period display script | Halftone, punchy |
| Newsprint editorial | Cream, deep red, mustard and charcoal, condensed headline | Heavy halftone and aged paper, punchy |

Build a custom theme by choosing a medium, era, layout, two or three colors, a named type style, print finish and lighting. Try a scanned-flat or overhead view, with subtle shadows between cutouts. Keep one focal hierarchy and use negative space around the headline.

## Image prompt in five parts

1. **Style block** reused word for word across shots. “Mixed-media hand-cut paper collage. Torn newsprint edges, tape corners, halftone dots, scissor-cut silhouettes, shallow paper shadows, straight-on scanned light.”
2. **Scene as separate pieces.** “Wide view of a dock. Cutout crane in the middle, cargo container below, paper waves behind it, foreground rail at the bottom. Give each piece a visible edge and a distinct shadow.”
3. **Background.** “Bold flat cream paper. Deep red on the container, charcoal for the crane, mustard for the signal flag.”
4. **Headline space.** “Empty torn-paper banner in the upper right with room for a short title.” For an incidental baked headline, quote the exact two or three words and review the spelling. For names, dates, labels or other exact words, use native text. Keep the detail shot free of headlines.
5. **Framing.** Specify the actual aspect, subject placement and safe margins. Use a supported resolution that leaves the cutouts crisp on the planned crop.

A piece with its own edge and shadow can read as a separate depth plane. Fix blended silhouettes at the still stage before buying animation. Carry the accepted still as an image reference when the selected model supports it.

## Motion prompt in five axes

1. **Camera.** Choose one move and its amplitude, such as a shallow flat pan or a slow five-percent push.
2. **Element motion.** Name the rigid pieces that move, their direction and where they settle. Give each wide a lead action and secondary motion. Let a detail focus on one strong action.
3. **Aesthetic.** Preserve the paper edge, halftone, drop shadows and original layout.
4. **Feel.** Name the intended pace or mood, such as patient, curious or triumphant.
5. **Color.** Pin the palette and keep contrast stable.

> Straight-on wide in one continuous lateral move. The crane hook lifts and settles, the paper waves drift more slowly, and the tape corners stay pinned. Keep the cutouts flat and rigid, preserve the printed grain and empty title space, and hold the warm cream and deep red palette. Curious, measured pacing. Land in a steady final pose.

Describe what moves. Keep critical text as a separate stable layer. Use a cut to create a snap or punch between shots. Motion prompts are an artistic direction. If parts must land exactly or the label must stay unchanged, build the parts as independent layers.
