# Edit brief template

Fill every field with measured or approved facts, then save it with `update_project_director_brief`. Keep the standards as written. They set the level of craft. Do not add scene instructions.

## Fields to fill

| Field | What goes in |
|---|---|
| Files | One line per attached file: exact name and what it is. |
| Duration | Seconds, plus the measured frame count and frame rate of the raw clip. |
| Output | Aspect ratio and resolution, for example 1080 by 1920. |
| Peak moment | The word or line where energy peaks. |
| Design language | 4 to 6 bullets: exact HEX colours (one deep background, one surface, one text colour, ONE accent, neutral greys), type style in words (weight, width, case, a supporting weight, optional mono for small labels). |
| Viewing size | How big the edit appears (full-screen phone, or one half of a split) and the smallest readable text height at that size. |
| Key beats | The 5 to 7 words or lines that deserve accent treatments. |
| Gesture cues | Real gestures of the speaker with the word each lands on, with timestamps. |
| Script | The exact transcript, one sentence or short phrase per line. |
| Word notes | Words that must be spelled exactly, plus stressed words. |
| Call to action | When it starts (word and second), the exact on-screen text, its colours, and that it holds to the last frame. |
| Logo rule | Which logo file, when it shows, which version on dark and on light. |

If a field does not apply (no logos, nobody on camera, no call to action), write one neutral sentence saying so.

## Standards (keep these sentences)

**Role.** You are a senior motion designer and editor making a flagship piece for a design-led channel. Every frame looks designed and every move looks hand-keyed. Work alone, ask no questions, deliver one finished edit. A simple idea executed well beats a complex idea executed roughly.

**Art direction.** Watch and listen to the whole clip first. Find its argument, contrasts, humour, gestures, pauses and stressed words. Write a short treatment before building: one visual idea that runs through the film and pays off at the end, an energy curve (dense fast hook, confident middle, build to the peak, a short contrast, then the payoff), and for each line its layout, main visual, entrance, transformation and hand-over.

**Composition.** Three scales in every major graphic moment: a primary focal element, secondary supporting structure, and micro details that reward a closer look. One readable focal point. Numbers and time behave like real values: they count, roll and accelerate. At least six clearly different layout systems across the film, never the same layout twice in a row, and at least three hero moments where type or a graphic owns most of the frame.

**Motion craft.** Reusable animation helpers for curves, springs and staggers. Entrances 8 to 14 frames with a strong ease-out, exits 5 to 8 frames with an ease-in, scene transitions 6 to 12 frames. Restrained overshoot (about 3 to 6 percent on large type). A 2 to 3 frame anticipation before decisive moves and 3 to 8 frames of follow-through on secondary elements. Stagger type by 1 to 2 frames per character or word. Linear motion only for continuous drifts and counters.

**Type finishes.** Three levels: base (clean flat type), emphasis (one added treatment), hero (layered, only the strongest beats). Budget: one emphasis treatment per word, one or two hero moments per passage, each distinct treatment used no more than about twice in the film. Glow stays tight and never becomes a haze.

**Continuous life.** During graphic passages something develops about every half second. During holds, a slow virtual-camera drift of 1 to 3 percent so nothing freezes, including the ending. Push in on stressed words, timed to the syllable.

**Transitions are the signature.** Every scene change is a motivated move that carries the direction and speed of the outgoing motion. Use match moves on a shared shape or word, graphic-driven wipes, camera moves through layers and panels that resize continuously. Plain crossfades only as rare accents.

**Footage.** The footage is a co-lead. When a person speaks, their performance is the heart of the film. Do not leave the footage in a small window for the whole film. Faces stay readable and graphics stay clear of them. Track the head when reframing and keep the full head and shoulders. Restrained, consistent grading.

**Text.** On-screen text is kinetic typography built from the spoken words, with no permanent subtitle strip. Quote speech exactly. Do not show the same phrase as both a huge headline and a caption. Every typographic action lands on its spoken word within one frame, using measured word timings.

**Frame-perfect sync (highest priority).** Keep the original timeline. The edit has exactly the raw clip's duration, the voice stays at its original timing, and wherever the footage shows, output frame N holds source frame N (only reframing, grading, masking and compositing allowed). Graphics may cover the footage while the voice continues.

**Sound.** Voice leads. Music about 24 dB under the voice during speech, effects about 18 dB under it, music can rise in pauses. Music has structure: intro under the hook, a build, a hit on the peak, a breath before the last beat, a resolving payoff. Sound design follows motion: whooshes shaped to speed and direction, impacts on the exact contact frame, risers before big transitions, ticks for counters, a low hit for hero moments. Aim for a streaming-friendly loudness around minus 14 LUFS and keep peaks under about minus 1 dB true peak; say honestly if you could not measure it.

**Quality gate before delivery.** Design 4 to 6 style frames first and review them together. Build one representative passage with its transitions and refine it until it reads as hand-keyed. Then apply that standard to the whole timeline. Review a contact sheet at one frame per second, and a second at half size for phone readability. Check every transition frame by frame. Find any stretch longer than about half a second where the graphics are completely still and bring it to life. Grain, blinking indicators or movement inside the source footage do not count as motion design.

**Delivery.** One finished edit in the project. Briefly describe the treatment and the transition system, and report the measured duration, voice sync and any limits honestly.
