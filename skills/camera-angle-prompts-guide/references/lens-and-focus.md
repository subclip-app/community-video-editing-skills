# Lens and focus effects

Lens tricks, focus pulls and speed moves that change how the frame feels. Prompt wording is ours; [brackets] are placeholders. Paste each sentence into the camera block of the prompt formula (`prompt-formula.md`).

## 21. Whip pan

**Feeling:** A fast blur that slams the viewer into the next moment.

**Camera sentence:** The camera rests on [a window] in [place], then whips fast to the right with heavy motion blur and lands clean on [subject], already looking at the lens.

**Tip:** Start on something else so the camera has somewhere to whip from, and ask for heavy motion blur.

**In Subclip:** Native: two clips with a fast sideways position keyframe, a strong blur on the join, and a whoosh from search_public_audio. Often better than generating.

## 27. Rack focus

**Feeling:** Focus moves from one thing to another and the eye follows.

**Camera sentence:** The frame opens sharp on [a foreground lamp] with [subject] soft behind it; the focus pulls smoothly from the lamp to the face of [subject] as they turn to the lens.

**Tip:** Say what starts sharp and what the focus pulls to. A clear start and end point makes it work.

**In Subclip:** Native: stack two copies, animate blur on the top one down to 0 while the bottom one blurs up. Works best on a locked clip.

## 30. Crash zoom

**Feeling:** A sudden fast punch-in that grabs attention: shock or comedy.

**Camera sentence:** A wide view of [subject] on [seat] in [place], then a sudden fast zoom punches in to a tight close up of the face as they raise an eyebrow at the lens.

**Tip:** Ask for a sudden fast zoom from wide to close. A zoom, not a dolly, gives the snap.

**In Subclip:** Native and better: keyframe scale from 100 to 200 percent over 4 to 6 frames with a hit sound effect.

## 32. Dolly zoom

**Feeling:** Vertigo: the background stretches while the subject keeps the same size.

**Camera sentence:** [Subject] stands still in the middle of [place], the face held at the same size, while the camera pulls back and zooms in at once so the walls and window stretch and warp behind them.

**Tip:** Say the face stays the same size while the camera pulls back and zooms in at once.

**In Subclip:** Generate it. An honest native fake needs a separate background layer scaled the other way.

## 33. Barrel roll

**Feeling:** Pure energy: the whole world turns over and lands back upright.

**Camera sentence:** A slow push in toward [subject] who faces the lens in the middle of [place]; the camera rolls one full turn around its own lens axis, the whole frame rotating together, and settles level on the face.

**Tip:** Models tend to orbit instead of roll. Generate a steady push in, then spin it in the edit.

**In Subclip:** Native and better: a rotation keyframe from 0 to 360 over a push-in clip, scaled up so the corners stay covered.

## 34. Fisheye

**Feeling:** Playful and in your face: ultra wide glass bends the room around the subject.

**Camera sentence:** An ultra wide fisheye lens held inches from the face of [subject] in [place]; the nose and grin bulge toward the lens while walls and ceiling curve around them.

**Tip:** Ask for a lens very close to the face. Distance is what makes the bulge read as fisheye.

**In Subclip:** Native: the fisheye or barrelDistortion effect with a close crop (check list_element_catalog).

## 39. Split diopter

**Feeling:** Two worlds in focus at once: near and far both sharp.

**Camera sentence:** A [mug] sits sharp in the near foreground on [table] and [subject] sits sharp on [seat] in the background of [place], with a soft blurred band between them; they lift their eyes to the lens.

**Tip:** Name both subjects, one near and one far, say both are sharp and add a soft band between.

**In Subclip:** Generate it. A cut-out of the foreground over a blurred midground can fake it if you have a clean plate.
