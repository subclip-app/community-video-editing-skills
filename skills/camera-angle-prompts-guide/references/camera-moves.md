# Camera moves

The camera travels, rises, turns or glides during the shot. Prompt wording is ours; [brackets] are placeholders. Paste each sentence into the camera block of the prompt formula (`prompt-formula.md`).

## 15. Tracking

**Feeling:** The camera travels sideways with the subject at the same pace.

**Camera sentence:** The camera tracks sideways alongside [subject] as they walk slowly past [background element] in [place], plants and shelves sliding by behind; they glance at the lens.

**Tip:** Say which way they walk and that the camera moves with them at the same speed.

**In Subclip:** Native: keyframe position across an oversized clip (scale 115 percent) to drift with the walk. Generate for a real parallax.

## 16. Dolly in

**Feeling:** A steady glide forward to add emphasis and pull the viewer in.

**Camera sentence:** [Subject] stands by [window] in [place] and the camera glides steadily forward toward them, from a medium shot to a close up; they hold the gaze.

**Tip:** Ask for a smooth glide, not a zoom. Start wider than you want to finish.

**In Subclip:** Native: a scale keyframe from 100 to 130 percent with ease in and out. Cheap and effective.

## 17. Dolly out

**Feeling:** A pull back that reveals the world: ideal for endings.

**Camera sentence:** The camera glides smoothly back from [subject], revealing [the sofa, lamp and rest of the place] while they stand still and look into the lens.

**Tip:** Say what gets revealed so the model knows where to stop.

**In Subclip:** Native: scale keyframe from 130 down to 100 percent. Needs margin in the source, so start from a close shot.

## 18. Orbit

**Feeling:** The hero move: an arc around the subject that adds dimension.

**Camera sentence:** The camera arcs in a smooth half circle at chest height around [subject] in [place], [lamp, window and shelves] sliding through the background; they turn their head to follow the lens.

**Tip:** Ask for a half circle at chest height and let the subject follow the lens with their head.

**In Subclip:** Generate it. Native fakes of an orbit look flat.

## 19. Crane

**Feeling:** A rising reveal that lifts the story to a bigger scale.

**Camera sentence:** The camera rises from waist height up toward the high ceiling of [place], looking down at [subject] standing on the rug; they tip the head back and look up at the lens.

**Tip:** State the start height and the end height. Waist to ceiling is a clean, readable move.

**In Subclip:** Native: a downward position keyframe on an oversized clip fakes a small rise. Generate for a true crane.

## 20. Drone

**Feeling:** Aerial scale: a sweeping high move that opens up the whole space.

**Camera sentence:** A sweeping high move: the camera pulls back and up, revealing all of [place] below while [subject] looks up at the lens.

**Tip:** It works indoors as well as outside. Ask for a move up and back so the space opens out below.

**In Subclip:** Generate it. Aerial parallax cannot be faked on a flat clip.

## 28. Tilt reveal

**Feeling:** A slow build: the camera climbs the body and the face lands last.

**Camera sentence:** The camera starts on [the shoes] of [subject] on the rug in [place] and tilts slowly up past the hands and chest to land on the face as they look down into the lens.

**Tip:** Give a start and an end point: start on the shoes, land on the face.

**In Subclip:** Native: a downward position keyframe on a tall oversized clip (a full-body source works well).

## 41. Handheld

**Feeling:** Real and urgent: a loose camera that breathes with the moment.

**Camera sentence:** A loose hand-held camera with visible shake and small drifts in the framing follows [subject] in close up as they talk to the lens in [place], the frame breathing with the operator.

**Tip:** Ask for visible shake and small drifts. Gentle handheld can look locked off.

**In Subclip:** Native: tiny keyframed position and rotation moves (1 to 2 percent) every few frames, or a shake effect if the catalog has one.

## 42. Steadicam

**Feeling:** Smooth and immersive: the camera glides with the character like a ghost.

**Camera sentence:** The camera glides smoothly behind [subject] as they walk through [place], then sweeps round to their face as they turn to the lens.

**Tip:** Describe the path: start behind, glide round to the face. A clear path keeps it smooth.

**In Subclip:** Generate it. Native fakes cannot change the point of view.

## 43. Pedestal up

**Feeling:** A steady reveal: the camera rises straight up without tilting.

**Camera sentence:** The camera starts level on the chest of [subject] in [place] and rises straight up without tilting, revealing the face as they look into the lens.

**Tip:** Say it rises vertically and stays level, or the model will tilt up instead.

**In Subclip:** Native: a downward position keyframe on an oversized clip with no rotation.

## 44. Slow pan

**Feeling:** Calm and observational: the camera turns on the spot to find the subject.

**Camera sentence:** The camera starts on [a tall window] in [place] and pans slowly across the room until it lands on [subject] standing by [lamp], who looks into the lens.

**Tip:** Give a start point and an end point so the pan has a journey.

**In Subclip:** Native: a sideways position keyframe on an oversized clip. Good for a free pan across a wide still image.
