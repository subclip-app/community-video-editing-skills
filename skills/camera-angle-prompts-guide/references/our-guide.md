# Our guide: choosing and pairing camera angles

Written by Subclip. It uses the 44 angle names as a vocabulary and adds the choosing logic.

## 1. Choose by what the shot must do

Ask one question per shot: what should the viewer feel or learn? Then pick the angle from the table.

| Emotion or job | Angles to try | Use case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trust, honesty | Eye level (1), Medium shot (9), Close up (7) | Talking head, testimonial, founder story |
| Power, confidence | Low angle (2), Worm's eye (5), Ground level (35), Cowboy shot (23) | Hero intro, product launch, sports |
| Vulnerability, doubt | High angle (3), Bird's eye (4) | Reflective beat, problem moment |
| Intimacy, intensity | Close up (7), Extreme close up (8), Macro (40), Insert (24) | Emotion, detail, food, tech close-ups |
| Place and scale | Wide shot (11), Extreme wide (12), Drone (20), Crane (19) | Opening, travel, real estate |
| Tension, unease | Dutch angle (13), Hidden camera (29), Dolly zoom (32), Handheld (41) | Thriller, mystery, ad hook |
| Energy, surprise | Whip pan (21), Crash zoom (30), Barrel roll (33), Fisheye (34) | Transitions, comedy, social hooks |
| Reveal | Tilt reveal (28), Pedestal up (43), Dolly out (17), Slow pan (44) | Product reveal, ending, big moment |
| Following, immersion | Tracking (15), Steadicam (42), POV (14), Snorricam (31) | Walk and talk, tour, action |
| Connection, conversation | Over the shoulder (6), Profile (22), Dolly in (16) | Dialogue, interview |
| Hero beauty | Orbit (18), Rack focus (27), Split diopter (39), Reflection (25) | Product shot, character moment |

## 2. Set order that reads as directed

- Open wide to place the viewer (11, 12 or 20).
- Move to medium for the idea (9 or 23), then close for the feeling (7 or 8).
- Add one inserted detail (24 or 40) to break up faces.
- Put one unusual angle in the middle, never two in a row (for example 5, 14 or 29).
- Finish calm: eye level (1), a dolly out (17) or a slow pan (44).
- Three or four different angles in one minute already looks designed. Twelve does not; it looks random.

## 3. Pairing angles into a cut

Good pairs, with a reason:

- **Wide then close up.** Place, then feeling. The classic.
- **Low angle then high angle.** Power flips to vulnerability when the story turns.
- **Medium then insert.** The idea, then the object that proves it.
- **Dolly in then crash zoom cut.** A slow build and then a hit on the punchline.
- **Over the shoulder then reverse over the shoulder.** A conversation from both sides.
- **Whip pan out, whip pan in.** Hides a cut between places or times.
- **Tilt reveal then close up.** The same body, then the face lands.
- **Drone then ground level.** From the world into the scene.

Avoid pairing: two moves in opposite directions back to back (dolly in then dolly out) without a still shot between; two near-identical distances (medium then medium) which feels like a jump cut.

## 4. Hold the continuity

- Keep the subject, outfit, place and light identical across the set: same reference images and the same grade block.
- Change one thing per shot: either the height, the distance or the move.
- Match direction: if the subject looks to the right in the first shot, keep it unless the reverse is the point.
- Cut on action: end a clip as the subject starts a move and start the next clip in that move.

## 5. Generate or fake?

- Cheap and good natively: crops (close up, medium, cowboy, insert), dolly in and out (scale keyframes), crash zoom, barrel roll, whip pan, slow pan, pedestal up, handheld, fisheye.
- Needs a model: any new viewpoint (worm's eye, bird's eye, over the shoulder, POV, orbit, crane, drone, Snorricam, steadicam, dolly zoom, split diopter).
- When in doubt, fake first, generate only what the fake cannot do. Credits are saved for the shots that earn them.
