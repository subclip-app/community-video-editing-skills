# Opener concepts

Adapted from Bang Tutorial's concept library. Choose a structure before choosing effects. A new palette on the same hook / three features / promise / logo / CTA sequence is still a template.

## Choose and record

Propose three concepts, each with a one-sentence premise about this product. Recommend the one whose benefit is most convincing when seen. Record: concept / beat count / main objects / opening / ending. Compare with any previous opener known from this session or the creator's brief. Change the concept or at least three fingerprint fields.

Use examples as principles. Invent a different opening and ending and transform any signature moment through the brand's meaning. At most two familiar components should appear unless the creator specifically requires more: zooming headline exiting left, three feature tiles, typed search box, camera push-through, cut light leak, centered logo plus CTA. Each needs a reason.

Two concepts may combine, but choose one as the structural parent. Segment a continuous world into short Subclip layers with matched boundary states; conceptual continuity does not require one huge source file.

## Expanded recipes

### Object relay

Map the actual service journey: need, choose, gather, process, arrive. Delete generic steps. A hero can change from screen item to card to object to parcel while retaining silhouette or color. Use props the brand sells. An unexpected beneficiary, even a paw taking delivery, can be the actor. Brand color stays on the important object while environments change. Branding may live on packaging or walls. The ending can be receipt of the object rather than an end card.

Plan a dominant action per chapter. Opening and arrival may last about two seconds, choice and processing about three to four. Let meaning determine the times.

### Brand system

Build with the creator's real symbol, type and applications. Use generous empty space, a central object about 25 to 35 percent of frame height, and stickers around its corners. Keep most surfaces light; one or two full brand color fields feel stronger than constant saturation. Large brand-guide pages can be treated as objects, but exact labels must remain readable when the story needs them. A loop may end where the opening motif began. Do not copy the source example's chapter order.

### Claim, method, result

Use product UI typography, a warm near-white layered surface and color primarily inside the product's outputs. Claims take about one to 2.5 seconds, method two to six, result four to six. UI floats without a device frame. A pointer drags an asset, selects an option, then the result expands to occupy most of the frame. Its container can contract to a dot that anchors the next claim. Reveal words with opacity and transforms, with no text blur or filtered parent.

### Living ecosystem

Make one real workflow the spine: assemble, act, process, result. Roles and integrations are optional evidence, not a checklist. Use a floating panel for core work and reserve native device shots for a major reveal or closing moment. Integration logos need truthful product support and approved use. Otherwise use generic file-type icons. Keep UI terminology and data grounded in the creator's product.

A calm 50 to 60 second tour may use twelve to sixteen short beats, mostly connected by UI changes. This is a scale example, not a mandatory length. Small claims stand beside the UI rather than covering it.

### Flat pop with photos

Derive flat containers and stickers from logo geometry. People can pop from the bottom, lean out of a frame or pose across a brand letter. Change color fields because a toggle flips, a symbol fills the lens or a cut lands on a beat. A large low-contrast word behind a person can carry a role. Match the person's position across fast photo changes. Use exact brand text as editable type.

Prepare a shot list: pose, crop, expression, role, orientation and clean text space. Prefer creator photos, then approved licensed media or generated fictional subjects. Keep one style and persona per role. A useful prompt:

> Studio photograph of a fictional [role], [pose and expression], [approved clothing colors], consistent soft lighting, plain contrasting background, [full or half body], generous room on [side] for an editable title. No text or logos.

Use Subclip generation and imports from subclip-production.md. Do not regenerate real employees or public figures as evidence. For cutouts, inspect hair edges and halos at close view. Keep enough resolution for the largest close-up, ideally 1.5 to two times the displayed pixel size. Put exact logos over the image as independent graphics.

Animate with a small overshoot and rotation, two or three parallax depths, and foreground stickers. A head can extend beyond a photo container using a carefully masked duplicate. Avoid duplicate audio or independently playing footage. A tiny Ken Burns move alone does not make a photo campaign feel alive.

### Through gradient forms

Choose one meaningful opening, such as a keyhole, letter or portal. Forms can occupy 80 to 120 percent of the local stage height. A guide light about three to six percent tall gives the eye an anchor. Use two or three palette colors inside the shapes and a deep base, with fine deterministic grain. Layers may rotate 10 to 20 degrees per second while camera moves last about 0.8 to 1.2 seconds. Reserve the fastest motion for the guide light. The reveal must come from this brand, not a copied flower or tunnel sequence.

### Product tour with claims

Alternate a task with a claim. Show the UI at two scales: whole task view and lifted detail card. A card can detach while the dashboard dims and the color field emerges behind it. A split can put the claim on one side and UI on the other, then close toward the next button. Use brand action colors so the palette change feels caused by the product. One or two cuts can mark chapters. Avoid two claims in a row without product evidence.

## UI choreography

Rebuild only the parts of the actual UI needed to tell the task, using DOM/SVG inside a HyperFrames segment. Keep important labels and controls accurate. Small irrelevant copy can become gray lines. If UI does not exist, identify the visualization as a proposed interface rather than verified product behavior.

- Assemble heading, input and options, then let a pointer, finger or autonomous UI action use them.
- Pointer arrives with ease-out, pauses about 0.2 seconds, button compresses to about 0.92 scale, then the result appears.
- For an important click, move toward its target 0.3 to 0.5 seconds before arrival. A local scale around 1.4 to 2.2 over 0.6 to 0.9 seconds is a starting range. Move back or toward the result in about 0.7 to one second. Avoid oscillating faster than viewers can read.
- Data fills progressively: bars stagger by about 0.08 seconds, progress advances, a counter derives from local time.
- Keep a pointer within the same UI camera group so it scales with the button.
- Expand the same panel into a next step; lift a result card; flip an icon to change its meaning; keep a container while its content changes.
- Use a native 3D layer for a phone or laptop reveal, not a device hidden inside motion code.

## Menus for a fresh structure

Feature alternatives: one full demonstration, a word replaced in a sentence, a transforming object, shuffled cards, icons orbiting a product, a checklist completed by world action, before/after divider, a collected pile, an opening folder releasing contents, a role portrait, a guidebook page, a claim/result cycle, or a UI widget becoming a diagram. Tile grids are one choice, with a count derived from the product.

Openings: action already underway, sound before image, an oversized cropped word that resolves, a drawing that becomes the logo, a material close-up, a question on an object, one light revealing the world, immediate disorder, a monochrome world with one colored actor, or an unexpected beneficiary.

Endings: logo formed from the final object, pull-back revealing a mark, logo replacing the final word, an in-world button being used, a seamless callback, a deliberate still hold, delivery received, a product result held onscreen, or the last word resolving into a brand name. Centered logo plus CTA belongs only when the brief calls for it.

Object-led transitions: a meaningful next object fills the lens; a card becomes a physical object; a shape match changes material; a hand collects the hero; a palette field stays as the next background; a container expands; a result contracts to a dot; an icon flips edge-on; a lifted widget becomes a diagram; a photo card becomes the next scene; a brand mask opens; a guide light leads to the next detail. Choose a few that serve the story.
